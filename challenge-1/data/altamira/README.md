# Altamira: the Nova Aliança clearing

*A fictional environmental enforcement operation, built on the real forms and
real procedure of the Altamira municipal environment secretariat (SEMMA). Every
person, property, document number and coordinate is invented.*

## Where this happens

Altamira, in the state of Pará, is the largest municipality in Brazil by area.
Its rural roads are called **travessões**, numbered side roads that run off the
main highways into the forest. Cell coverage ends a few kilometres in. An
inspection team that leaves the city in the morning is offline for the rest of
the day, and everything they record has to survive until they get back.

The municipal secretariat, **SEMMA**, has four inspectors and one vehicle for a
territory the size of Portugal. Satellite deforestation alerts arrive monthly,
in a bulletin, and the team picks which ones they can physically reach.

## What happened

On **12 March 2026** a four-person SEMMA team drove 36 km to kilometre 14 of
Travessão da 27, in the Assurini district. They were checking a deforestation
alert from February's satellite bulletin, reinforced by an anonymous complaint
filed three weeks earlier by a neighbour who said a bulldozer had been working
the area and that a large fire had covered the road in smoke one Saturday
night.

They arrived at 08:02 and found a **crawler dozer still warm**, parked in the
middle of a fresh clearing. The man operating it said he only worked there.
Over the next two hours the team walked the perimeter on foot, marking four
corners with the phone's GPS, and measured the cleared native vegetation. Part
of it ran all the way to the bank of the Santa Rosa creek, destroying the strip
of riverside forest that Brazilian law protects absolutely. Another part of it
had been burned. They also found an unregistered chainsaw leaning against a
stump.

The owner arrived by motorcycle at 10:08. He produced no licence, no clearing
authorisation and no chainsaw registration, and said the land had been his
father's and nobody had ever objected. At 10:35 the inspector filled in the
**finding notice** by hand, on the hood of the truck. The owner listened to it
being read out and **refused to sign**; the inspector wrote the refusal into
the signature box, which is what the procedure requires.

The dozer and the chainsaw stayed where they were. The team had no trailer.

Five days later, on 17 March, the secretariat's board met and authorised the
penalty. Two more documents were issued that afternoon: a **fine**, calculated
at a fixed rate per hectare, and an **embargo** shutting the area down. The
written report closing the case was signed on 19 March.

## Why the paperwork is shaped the way it is

This is the part that matters for the challenge. Altamira's documents form a
**chain, and each link names the one before it by number**:

| Document | Issued | Names |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint record | 24 Feb, office | nothing |
| **Finding notice** | **12 Mar, in the field, by hand** | nothing |
| Infraction notice (the fine) | 17 Mar, office | the finding notice, by number |
| Embargo notice | 17 Mar, office | the infraction notice, by number |
| Inspection report | 19 Mar, office | all three, by number |

The printed form for the fine literally contains the line *"in accordance with
finding notice number ____, issued by ____"*. So the numbers are not
decoration: they are how a case holds together, and how an auditor checks it.

Notice also that **only one document exists at the moment of the occurrence**.
Everything else is written days later, at a desk, after a board meeting. That
is why the field package you receive contains the finding notice and nothing
else.

## What you get

- **Five documents** as photographs, the way they reach the office: the
  handwritten field notice, the two office-issued notices, the complaint record
  and the report.
- **Four field photographs** with GPS, date and time burned into the pixels by
  the app, in varying quality, because the person holding the phone is working.
- **Ten voice notes** dictated during the day, from the drive out to the closing.
- **The occurrence summary** exactly as the Sumaúma app exports it.

Two things are worth knowing before you start. The dates are **not all the
same**: 12 March is the event, 17 March is the penalty. And the coordinates in
the paperwork carry a decimal of a second, while the app rounds the second to a
whole number. Both describe the same point.
